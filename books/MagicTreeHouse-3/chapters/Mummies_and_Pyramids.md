## Chapter 1: Ancient Egypt

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 10](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-010-img-01.jpg)

For thousands of years, mummies and pyramids were a great mystery. How were the pyramids built? Why did people make mummies? What was the strange writing on mummy cases?

<!-- PDF page 11 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 11](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-011-img-01.jpg)

In the last 200 years, scientists have learned a great deal about the people who built the pyramids and made mummies.

They were a hardworking people who enjoyed life. They loved science and music. They believed in a sun god and a cat goddess. They thought they could live forever.

They were the people of ancient Egypt.

<!-- PDF page 12 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 12](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-012-img-01.jpg)

A person who studies ancient Egypt is called an Egyptologist (EE-jip-TOL-uh-jist).

Egypt is where one of the oldest civilizations in the world began. Five thousand years ago, Egyptians invented one of the first forms of writing. They made a kind of paper from reed plants. They invented a calendar much like the ones we use today.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 12](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-012-img-02.jpg)

A civilization is a group of people with an advanced way of life that includes science, art, and most often writing.

The ancient Egyptians built some of the most amazing buildings the world has ever known. Their painters and sculptors created beautiful works of art. Their doctors went to school to study how the body works.

Why did such a great civilization begin in Egypt so long ago? Most historians think the answer is simple: the Nile River. The Nile River is the longest river in the world. It flows through the middle of Egypt.

<!-- PDF page 13 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 13](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-013-img-01.jpg)

The Nile gave the ancient Egyptians water for drinking and bathing. Egyptian fishermen caught many kinds of fish in the Nile. Hunters hunted wild birds along its banks. Boats sailed up and down the Nile, carrying people and goods.

<!-- PDF page 14 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 14](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-014-img-01.jpg)

But the Nile’s greatest gift to the Egyptians wasn’t fish or birds or drinking water or travel. It was mud!

### Floods and Farming

Most of the land in Egypt is part of the Sahara Desert. Desert soil is sandy and rocky. It is not good for farming.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 14](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-014-img-02.jpg)

The Sahara is the biggest desert in the world!

The ancient Egyptians called the desert the Red Land. No one lived in the Red Land. Hardly anything could grow there.

The land along the banks of the Nile was very different from the Red Land. The soil there was dark and soft. The Egyptians called this land the Black Land. The Black Land was some of the best farmland in the world.

Why was the Black Land so good for farming? Every July, the Nile flooded its banks. The floodwater dumped a layer of black mud along each side of the river.

The black mud was very fertile (FUR-tul). That means it was full of the things that plants need to grow.

<!-- PDF page 15 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 15](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-015-img-01.jpg)

In November, the floodwaters went down. Farmers plowed the Black Land. They planted seeds in the rich, fertile soil.

In March, they harvested their crops. The harvest was almost always very plentiful. That means there was more than enough food for everyone.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 15](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-015-img-02.jpg)

To harvest means “to gather and store the crops you’ve grown.”

<!-- PDF page 16 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 16](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-016-img-01.jpg)

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 16](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-016-img-02.jpg)

### Two Kingdoms

Villages formed near the good farmland along the Nile. Some of the villages became cities. Over time, the villages and cities became part of two separate kingdoms.

For many years, each kingdom had a different king. One king ruled over the land in the north, where the Nile flows into the sea. The other king ruled over the land in the south, along the Nile valley.

About 5,000 years ago, a king named Menes (MEE-neez) united the two kingdoms.

<!-- PDF page 17 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 17](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-017-img-01.jpg)

#### To unite means “to join together.”

King Menes built a capital city close to where the separate kingdoms had met. He wore a special crown. It was made from the crowns of both kingdoms.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 17](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-017-img-02.jpg)

Most historians say the joining of the two kingdoms was the beginning of the great Egyptian nation. When King Menes put on his double crown, he started a civilization that lasted for 3,000 years.

<!-- PDF page 18 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 18](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-018-img-01.jpg)

<!-- PDF page 19 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 19](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-019-img-01.jpg)

The ancient Egyptians wrote with pictures. Their picture writing is called hieroglyphic (HY-ro-GLIF-ik) writing.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 19](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-019-img-02.jpg)

The pictures were mostly of things from everyday life.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 19](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-019-img-03.jpg)

But the pictures did not always stand for the thing they showed.

<!-- PDF page 20 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 20](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-020-img-01.jpg)

Most Egyptians never learned to read hieroglyphs. There were over 700 different signs!

## Chapter 2: Everyday Life

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 21](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-021-img-01.jpg)

Most ancient Egyptians were farmers. They lived in villages and towns in the Black Land along the Nile River. Their houses were made of mud bricks. The mud for the bricks was another gift of the Nile.

<!-- PDF page 22 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 22](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-022-img-01.jpg)

Even wealthy people lived in houses that were very close to each other. That’s because Egyptians wanted to save most of the Black Land for farming.

Egyptian houses usually had high, flat roofs. Families often did their cooking on the roof. They sometimes slept on the roof in the summer.

Poor families’ houses usually had only one room. Wealthy Egyptians had larger

<!-- PDF page 23 -->

houses. They had servants to do their cooking, washing, and other chores.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 23](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-023-img-01.jpg)

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 23](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-023-img-02.jpg)

Most Egyptians did not have a lot of furniture. Their houses had only a few stools, small tables, and floor mats. Walls were painted bright colors. Ceilings were high to help keep the house cool.

Egyptians slept on beds made of wood and reeds. They rested their heads on headrests. The headrests were usually carved from wood.

Egypt has a very warm climate. Most ancient Egyptians hardly ever wore shoes. Their clothing was mostly white and loose-fitting. Almost all their clothes were made of linen. Linen is a cloth made from flax plants.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 23](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-023-img-03.jpg)

#### Climate means “the usual weather of a place.”

Ancient Egyptians cared a great deal about how they looked. Wealthy Egyptian men and women both wore eye makeup and wigs. They also wore necklaces, bracelets, and rings.

<!-- PDF page 24 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 24](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-024-img-01.jpg)

Egyptians loved perfume. They liked to rub good-smelling oils and creams on their skin.

### Children and Family Life

Egyptologists believe the ancient Egyptians loved children. Egyptian art often shows parents having fun with their sons and daughters.

<!-- PDF page 25 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 25](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-025-img-01.jpg)

Egyptian children played with spinning tops and balls. Both boys and girls played with dolls and wooden animals.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 25](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-025-img-02.jpg)

Children and grownups also played board games. These games were sort of like checkers or chess.

<!-- PDF page 26 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 26](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-026-img-01.jpg)

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 26](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-026-img-02.jpg)

Most children did not go to school. They lived with their parents until they were married.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 26](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-026-img-03.jpg)

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 26](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-026-img-04.jpg)

The ancient Egyptians were some of the first people to keep animals as pets. The Egyptians loved their pets. They treated them like members of the family. One story says that when a family’s pet cat died, the whole family shaved off their eyebrows to show their grief.

<!-- PDF page 27 -->

### Artists and Craftspeople

There were many skilled artists and craftspeople in Egypt.

Sculptors and painters decorated the palaces and temples. Potters worked with clay to make bowls, jars, and statues.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 27](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-027-img-01.jpg)

Weavers wove cloth for clothes and bedding.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 27](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-027-img-02.jpg)

Shipbuilders built sailboats and barges for traveling up and down the Nile.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 27](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-027-img-03.jpg)

A barge is a boat with a flat bottom.

Other craftspeople made leather goods and jewelry.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 27](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-027-img-04.jpg)

<!-- PDF page 28 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 28](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-028-img-01.jpg)

### Scribes

One of the most important jobs in ancient Egypt was that of a scribe.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 28](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-028-img-02.jpg)

Scribes kept records for the government. They also kept records for merchants and traders. They copied down magic spells and scientific information.

It took years of schooling to become a scribe. The hieroglyphic alphabet was very hard to learn. Most Egyptians never learned it at all. They hired scribes to read and write for them.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 28](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-028-img-03.jpg)

Not fair! Only boys could go to school and become scribes!

<!-- PDF page 29 -->

### Pharaohs

The rulers of Egypt lived very differently from ordinary people. They had hundreds of servants. Their homes were grand palaces.

Egyptian rulers came to be called pharaohs (FAIR-roez). Pharaohs had total power over their people. Ancient Egyptians believed their pharaoh controlled the weather, the flooding of the Nile, and the growth of their crops.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 29](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-029-img-01.jpg)

#### Pharaoh means “great house.”

Egyptians thought their pharaoh was more than a person. They worshipped him as a god.

<!-- PDF page 30 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 30](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-030-img-01.jpg)

Crocodiles, hippos, and beautiful birds lived along the Nile River. Lions, wild bulls, and jackals also lived in ancient Egypt. Egyptian statues and jewelry were often made to look like animals.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 30](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-030-img-02.jpg)

## Chapter 3: Egyptian Religion

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 32](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-032-img-01.jpg)

Ancient Egyptians worshiped their pha-raoh. They also worshiped many gods and goddesses.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 32](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-032-img-02.jpg)

Egyptians pictured their gods and goddesses in several different ways. A few were like ordinary men and women. Some were like animals. Many were half-human and half-animal.

The Egyptians believed the gods and goddesses watched over everything they did.

### Temples

The Egyptians built great temples for their most important gods and goddesses. Inside the temples were sacred statues. Priests at the temples cared for the statues. They washed and dressed them. They even served them meals!

<!-- PDF page 33 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 33](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-033-img-01.jpg)

Ordinary people were not allowed to see the sacred statues inside the temples. When they visited, they said prayers and left gifts outside. At home, they prayed to their own statues of their favorite gods and goddesses.

### The Next Life

An important part of Egyptian religion was belief in a Next Life. The Next Life was where people went after they died. There they could enjoy many of the same things they had enjoyed on earth.

Egyptians believed that every person was made up of three parts. The first part was the body.

The second part was the ka. The ka was the person’s life force. It was what made the person alive.

The third part was the ba. The ba was what made the person different from anyone else.

Egyptians believed that when a person died, the ba and ka left the body. For the person to live in the Next Life, the ba and ka had to come together again.

<!-- PDF page 34 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 34](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-034-img-01.jpg)

The body was home for the ba and the ka. So it was very important that the body of a dead person not be destroyed.

To keep the body from being destroyed, the ancient Egyptians turned it into a mummy.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 34](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-034-img-02.jpg)

<!-- PDF page 35 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 35](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-035-img-01.jpg)

Ancient Egyptians worshiped many gods and goddesses. Here are some of the most important ones.

### RA (RAH)

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 35](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-035-img-02.jpg)

Ra was the sun god. He was sometimes shown with the body of a man and the head of a falcon. The Egyptians believed Ra created the world. They thought Ra sailed across the sky every day in a golden boat. At sunset, Ra sailed his boat into the underworld. The underworld was a kingdom beneath the earth. At sunrise, Ra rose from the underworld and sailed his boat across the sky again.

### OSIRIS AND ISIS (oh-SY-ris and I-sis)

<!-- PDF page 36 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 36](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-036-img-01.jpg)

Egyptians believed that long ago, Osiris and Isis had been the first king and queen of Egypt. When Osiris was murdered by his evil brother, Isis used magic powers to bring him back to life.

Osiris became god of the dead and ruler of the underworld. Isis became the goddess of healing, marriage, and motherhood.

### HORUS (HOR-us)

Horus was the son of Isis and Osiris. He was a falcon-headed god. Egyptians believed their pharaoh was Horus in human form.

<!-- PDF page 37 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 37](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-037-img-01.jpg)

### BASTET (BAST-ut)

<!-- PDF page 38 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 38](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-038-img-01.jpg)

Bastet was Ra’s daughter. She was a cat goddess. She was shown as a cat, or a woman with a cat’s head. Egyptians believed Bastet had the sun’s power to make their crops grow. They prayed to Bastet for a good harvest every year. Bastet was also the goddess of music and dance and the goddess of joy and love.

### THOTH (THOTH or TOTE)

<!-- PDF page 39 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 39](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-039-img-01.jpg)

Thoth was the moon god. Egyptians believed Thoth gave them the gift of writing. He was also the god of medicine and mathematics. He was sometimes shown as a baboon. He was also shown as a man with the head of a bird.

### BES (BES)

<!-- PDF page 40 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 40](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-040-img-01.jpg)

Bes was one of the Egyptians’ favorite gods. He was short, chubby, and happy. He had a lion’s ears and tail—and the body and face of a man. Bes brought joy and good luck to families. He protected the whole household.

## Chapter 4: Mummies

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 41](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-041-img-01.jpg)

Normally, when an animal or a person dies, the body begins to decay. That means that the skin, hair, muscles, and other parts of the body start to rot away. Finally, there is nothing left but bones.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 41](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-041-img-02.jpg)

A mummy is a dead body that has been protected from decay.

<!-- PDF page 42 -->

### The First Egyptian Mummies

The first Egyptian mummies were probably made by accident.

The ancient Egyptians needed the land near the Nile for farming. So they buried their dead in the nearby desert.

The hot desert sand dried out the bodies very quickly. They didn’t decay. They became mummies.

The Egyptians studied these desert mummies. They learned from them how to protect the body for the Next Life.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 42](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-042-img-01.jpg)

Oh, wow! Egyptologists think that the ancient Egyptians made over 70 million mummies!

### Making a Mummy

It took a long time to make a mummy. The work was done by a team of priests. Egyptians believed the god Anubis (uh-NOO-bis) watched over mummy making. Anubis had the body of a man and the head of a jackal. So the chief priest wore a mask of a jackal.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 42](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-042-img-02.jpg)

A jackal is a kind of wild dog that lives in Asia and North Africa.

The place where the priests worked was called the Beautiful House. Everything in the Beautiful House was done with great care.

First, all the organs except the heart were taken out of the body. The ancient Egyptians believed that the gods weighed a person’s heart when he or she tried to enter the Next Life. The weight of the heart would tell the gods if the person had lived a good life on earth.

Other organs were put in special jars to be buried with the mummy.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 42](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-042-img-03.jpg)

Oh, man! The ancient Egyptians didn’t know what a person’s brain was for—so they threw it away!

<!-- PDF page 43 -->

Next, the priests washed the body with wine. They said prayers. They rubbed the body with oils and spices.

Then the priests covered the body with a kind of salt called natron (NAY-tron). Natron worked like the desert sand, only better. It helped dry the body even more quickly and keep it from decaying.

Finally, the priests laid the body on a slanted table. They said more prayers. Then they left the body to dry for about forty days.

When the body had dried out, it was wrapped in strips of linen. The linen had been soaked in resin. As they dried, the linen strips became very hard.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 43](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-043-img-01.jpg)

Resin (REZ-in) is the sticky stuff that comes from trees or plants.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 43](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-043-img-02.jpg)

The priests wrapped magic charms in the mummy’s linen strips. The Egyptians believed these charms would bring good luck and protect the person in the Next Life.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 43](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-043-img-03.jpg)

Some mummies were buried with more than a hundred charms!

The priests wrapped and wrapped until the body was covered from head to toe. Sometimes a mask of the person’s face was placed over the linen strips. This was to help the ba and ka recognize the person underneath.

<!-- PDF page 44 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 44](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-044-img-01.jpg)

The layer of linen strips on some mummies was several inches thick.

When the body was fully wrapped, it was ready to be placed in its mummy case. There was usually a painting of the person’s face on the mummy case, too.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 44](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-044-img-02.jpg)

Sometimes the mummy’s case was placed in a stone coffin called a sarcophagus (sar-KAHF-uh-gus).

Many mummy cases were decorated with paintings and writing. The paintings often showed the person entering the Next Life and meeting the gods. The writing spelled out prayers and magic spells.

Early mummy cases were rectangular boxes, like this one:

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 44](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-044-img-03.jpg)

Later mummy cases were shaped more like the mummy inside:

<!-- PDF page 45 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 45](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-045-img-01.jpg)

When the mummy was sealed inside its case, it was ready to be taken to its tomb.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 45](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-045-img-02.jpg)

A tomb is a burial place.

<!-- PDF page 46 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 46](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-046-img-01.jpg)

The magic charms wrapped in the mummy’s linen strips are called amulets (AM-yuh-lits).

Some amulets were little statues of the gods.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 46](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-046-img-02.jpg)

Others were symbols, like a set of stairs leading to the Next Life.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 46](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-046-img-03.jpg)

Many amulets were in the shape of a beetle called a scarab (SKAAR-ub). The Egyptians believed that each day at dawn a giant scarab pushed the sun into the sky.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 46](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-046-img-04.jpg)

Scarab amulets were a symbol of rebirth in the Next Life. They often had wings. Another very popular amulet is called the Eye of Horus. The Egyptians believed the god Horus lost his eye in a fight with his evil uncle. It was magically put back by the god Thoth.

<!-- PDF page 47 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 47](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-047-img-01.jpg)

Egyptians thought that Eyes of Horus painted on mummy cases would allow mummies to see outside!

## Chapter 5: Egyptian Funerals

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 48](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-048-img-01.jpg)

Egyptian funerals were very big events. They were meant to send the dead person safely into the Next Life.

<!-- PDF page 49 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 49](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-049-img-01.jpg)

On the day a mummy was taken to its tomb, family and friends formed a funeral procession. The funeral procession was sort of like a parade.

The procession started at the dead person’s home. It included family members, friends, priests, and servants.

It was important to have a lot of people in the funeral procession. The dead person’s

<!-- PDF page 50 -->

family sometimes paid people to walk with them and mourn the person’s death. These people might not have even known the person in life. But they would scream and cry and throw dust on themselves to show their grief. If they did an especially good job of acting sad, they would be paid more!

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 50](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-050-img-01.jpg)

#### To mourn means “to show sadness.”

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 50](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-050-img-02.jpg)

The people in the funeral procession brought things they thought the dead person would need in the Next Life. They carried food and drink, weapons, tools, and musical instruments. If the person being buried was a child, they might even bring toys.

The funeral procession first went to the Beautiful House. There, the mummy in its mummy case was placed on a wooden barge. Oxen dragged the barge across the desert to the place where the mummy was to be buried.

As the procession crossed the sand, priests said prayers and recited magic spells. The ancient Egyptians believed it was important that everything be done right, or the person would never get to the Next Life!

<!-- PDF page 51 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 51](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-051-img-01.jpg)

When the procession arrived at the tomb, there was a special ceremony. It was called “The Opening of the Mouth.” A priest said a prayer. Then he touched the mummy’s mouth. Egyptians believed the Opening of the Mouth ceremony made it possible for the dead person to eat, drink, and speak in the Next Life.

Finally, the mummy was ready to be laid in its tomb.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 51](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-051-img-02.jpg)

<!-- PDF page 52 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 52](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-052-img-01.jpg)

<!-- PDF page 53 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 53](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-053-img-01.jpg)

Ancient Egyptians believed they would have to travel through the underworld to get to the Next Life. This journey was very dangerous!

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 53](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-053-img-02.jpg)

To help them make the journey safely, mummies were buried with prayers, magic spells, and maps of the underworld. These prayers and spells and maps were called the Book of the Dead.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 53](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-053-img-03.jpg)

The Book of the Dead wasn’t really a book. The spells and maps were written and drawn on papyrus scrolls.

<!-- PDF page 54 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 54](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-054-img-01.jpg)

The Egyptians believed that if the dead person followed the directions in the Book of the Dead and passed all the tests of the gods, he or she would live happily forever in the Next Life.

## Chapter 6: The Age of Pyramids

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 55](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-055-img-01.jpg)

Early pharaohs and other wealthy Egyptians had burial tombs made of mud bricks. These tombs are called mastabas (MAS-tuh-buhz). Mastabas looked like very large benches. They had flat roofs and slanted sides.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 55](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-055-img-02.jpg)

#### Mastaba is the Arabic word for “bench.”

<!-- PDF page 56 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 56](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-056-img-01.jpg)

The mummy wasn’t actually kept in the mastaba. It was buried underneath, in a room called a burial chamber. Sometimes the burial chamber was as much as eighty feet below the mastaba.

<!-- PDF page 57 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 57](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-057-img-01.jpg)

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 57](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-057-img-02.jpg)

### The First Pyramid

The first pyramid was created by an Egyptian architect and priest named Imhotep (im-HO-tep).

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 57](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-057-img-03.jpg)

An architect is a person who plans buildings.

Imhotep’s pharaoh was King Zoser (ZO-sur). King Zoser wanted the most wonderful burial place that had ever been built. So Imhotep planned a giant stone mastaba for King

<!-- PDF page 58 -->

Zoser. It would be the largest mastaba in all Egypt.

As the giant mastaba was being built, King Zoser decided he wanted something even better. Imhotep didn’t know what to do. He started to make the stone mastaba bigger. Then he got another idea.

Imhotep decided to put a smaller mastaba on top of the first one. He put another mastaba on top of that—then another and another. Finally, Zoser’s burial place was a stack of six mastabas.

Imhotep had created the first pyramid. Imhotep’s pyramid was nearly 200 feet high. After he died, the Egyptians worshiped Imhotep as one of their gods.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 58](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-058-img-01.jpg)

Step pyramids were built for several pharaohs who ruled after King Zoser. Then Egyptians began to build another kind of pyramid.

The new kind of pyramid started as a step pyramid. But once it was built, the steps were filled in to make the sides smooth.

Some people think this smooth-sided pyramid was made to look like a ray of sunlight. It is the pyramid shape we know best today.

### The Pyramids of Giza

The most famous Egyptian pyramids are the three smooth-sided Pyramids of Giza (GEE-zuh). They were built more than 4,500 years ago. All three are still standing today.

The largest of the Giza Pyramids is called the Great Pyramid. The Great Pyramid is nearly 500 feet tall.

<!-- PDF page 59 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 59](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-059-img-01.jpg)

The Great Pyramid was built for a pharaoh named Khufu (KOO-foo). In 1954, Egyptologists found a wooden boat buried near the Great Pyramid. The boat was over 140 feet long! They think it was probably the barge that led Khufu’s funeral procession and carried his mummy to its tomb.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 59](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-059-img-02.jpg)

The base of the Great Pyramid would cover ten football fields!

<!-- PDF page 60 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 60](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-060-img-01.jpg)

### Building the Pyramids

It took many years to build a pyramid. It also took thousands of workers.

Egyptologists think the workers used wooden rollers to move giant stones across the desert. Some of these stones weighed over 4,000 pounds!

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 60](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-060-img-02.jpg)

No one really knows exactly how the workers put the giant stones in place. Most Egyptologists think they built large ramps to raise the stones up the sides of the pyramid. When the pyramid was finished, the ramps were torn down.

Many people think the work on the pyramids was done by slaves. This is not true. Almost all the work was done by farmers during the flood season.

The farmers were paid to help build the pyramids. But most of them worked for a reason more important than money. They believed building the pyramids would help them get to the Next Life when they died.

<!-- PDF page 61 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 61](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-061-img-01.jpg)

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 61](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-061-img-02.jpg)

The Great Sphinx guards the Pyramids of Giza. A sphinx (SFINKS) is a make-believe creature with the body of a lion and the head of a hawk, a ram, or a person.

<!-- PDF page 62 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 62](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-062-img-01.jpg)

The Great Sphinx has the body of a lion and the head of a pharaoh named Khafre (KAH-fray). King Khafre’s pyramid is next to the Great Pyramid of King Khufu.

The Great Sphinx was carved from a rock mountain. It is the largest statue from ancient times still standing today.

## Chapter 7: Tomb Treasures and Tomb Robbers

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 63](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-063-img-01.jpg)

The ancient Egyptians wanted to be as comfortable and happy in the Next Life as they had been on earth. So they filled their tombs with everything they thought they might need.

<!-- PDF page 64 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 64](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-064-img-01.jpg)

Mummies were buried with extra clothes and sandals, writing materials, and fans. Their families put makeup, wigs, combs, and mirrors in the tombs. They wanted the dead to look their best in the Next Life.

Mummies were even buried with food and drink for the Next Life. Bread, beef, figs,

<!-- PDF page 65 -->

and jugs of beer have been found in their tombs.

### Shabtis

Wealthy people wanted to have servants in the Next Life. Since they couldn’t take their living servants with them, they made servant statues. These statues were called shabtis (SHAHB-teez).

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 65](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-065-img-01.jpg)

The Egyptians believed the shabtis would come to life—and go to work—in the Next Life.

There were often other statues in the tomb, too. Some were statues of the dead person. Egyptians believed these statues could be a home for the ba and ka if the mummy was destroyed. Other statues were there to guard the mummy and protect it from evil.

### Tomb Robbers

The items buried with mummies could be very valuable. Many amulets, statues, and mummy cases were partly made of gold. Some were decorated with jewels.

The mummies’ treasures were supposed to be used in the Next Life. But throughout history, many dishonest people have stolen the treasures to use in this life. These people are called tomb robbers.

<!-- PDF page 66 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 66](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-066-img-01.jpg)

Every pyramid standing today has been broken into by tomb robbers.

When tomb robbers broke into a tomb, they took everything of value. They showed no respect for the mummy or its sacred resting place.

Tomb robbers broke open mummy cases. They took the amulets from the mummy’s linen strips. They stole jewelry from the mummy’s body. They took the makeup and perfume and body oils that had been left for the mummy to use in the Next Life.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 66](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-066-img-02.jpg)

Sometimes tomb robbers even burned the mummy to light the tomb while they worked.

Tomb robbing was very dangerous. Ancient Egyptians thought tomb robbers were committing crimes against the gods. If they were caught, they were beaten and often put to death.

Sadly, this did not stop others from robbing the mummies’ tombs. Because of the tomb robbers’ greed, many great treasures of ancient Egypt have been lost forever.

<!-- PDF page 67 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 67](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-067-img-01.jpg)

One thing robbers couldn’t steal was the art on the walls of a tomb. Tomb paintings have taught us a great deal about life in ancient Egypt.

Tomb paintings often show people dancing or playing musical instruments. It was believed the dancers and musicians in the paintings would dance, sing, and play for the dead person in the Next Life.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 67](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-067-img-02.jpg)

Tomb paintings also show the person who had died. Even if the person was old and weak at the time of death, tomb paintings show him or her as young and healthy. Egyptians believed that was how the person would live in the Next Life.

## Chapter 8: The Most Famous Mummy of All

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 69](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-069-img-01.jpg)

Pyramids were built to honor pharaohs and their families. But pyramids also told tomb robbers exactly where to look for treasure.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 69](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-069-img-02.jpg)

Pharaohs cared more about keeping their mummies safe than about having huge pyramids. So they began to build secret tombs they hoped robbers couldn’t find.

<!-- PDF page 70 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 70](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-070-img-01.jpg)

### The Valley of the Kings

The Valley of the Kings is hidden deep in the Egyptian desert. It is surrounded by rocky cliffs with steep walls. There is a mountain near the Valley of the Kings that is shaped like a pyramid. The Egyptians thought this would be a perfect place to hide the tombs of their pharaohs.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 70](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-070-img-02.jpg)

The Egyptians called the Valley of the Kings the Great Place.

The people who built tombs in the Valley of the Kings worked very hard to keep them hidden. They cut secret chambers into the rock. They dug passageways that led nowhere. They built false entrances.

They sealed the real entrances with heavy stones. Guards watched the tombs day and night.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 70](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-070-img-03.jpg)

<!-- PDF page 71 -->

Still, tomb robbers found the tombs—and their treasures. More than sixty royal tombs were cut into the cliffs in the Valley of the Kings. Tomb robbers found them all.

### The Boy King’s Tomb

For many years, Egyptologists searched the Valley of the Kings for the tomb of a pharaoh named Tutankhamun (TOOT-ahn-KAH-mun).

Tutankhamun is sometimes called the “boy king.” He became pharaoh when he was nine years old. He died when he was only eighteen.

Egyptologists knew from ancient writings that Tutankhamun’s mummy had been buried in the Valley of the Kings. But for years, no one had been able to find his tomb.

One Egyptologist who worked very hard to find Tutankhamun’s tomb was Howard Carter. He searched for more than five years. He was about to give up—but then he made a discovery.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 71](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-071-img-01.jpg)

In 1922, Carter found stone steps buried beneath the ruins of some ancient workers’ huts.

Could these steps lead to the boy king’s tomb?

<!-- PDF page 72 -->

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 72](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-072-img-01.jpg)

Carter and his team began to dig. The steps led to a door. Behind the door was a tunnel. They kept digging.

Finally, Carter and his team came to what they thought must be the entrance to a tomb. Carter broke a hole in the door. He held a candle and peered through the hole.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 72](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-072-img-02.jpg)

“What do you see?” called someone in Carter’s team. “Wonderful things,” said Carter. Carter saw gold everywhere he looked: gold statues, gold boxes, gold chairs.

<!-- PDF page 73 -->

In another room, Carter found a huge stone sarcophagus. When he opened it, he found a mummy case. Inside was a second mummy case. Inside that was a third mummy case! The third mummy case was made of solid gold. And inside was the mummy of King Tutankhamun.

It took Carter and his team nearly ten years to study everything that was in Tutankhamun’s tomb. The tomb had been broken into during ancient times, but very little had been stolen. The robbers had probably been caught before they could get away with much.

The tomb of Tutankhamun is the most famous mummy discovery of all time. Tutankhamun’s treasures have been seen by millions of people all over the world. They are now in a museum in Cairo, Egypt—and King Tutankhamun’s mummy is resting safely back in its tomb in the Valley of the Kings.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 73](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-073-img-01.jpg)

Cairo (KY-ro) is the capital city of Egypt today.

## Chapter 9: Gifts of the Pyramids and Mummies

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 76](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-076-img-01.jpg)

Each year, millions of tourists visit the ancient pyramids in Egypt. There are thirty-five pyramids still standing along the banks of the Nile River.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 76](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-076-img-02.jpg)

The pyramids are empty now. Their mummies and treasures are in museums all over the world. There, they are treated with great care and respect. Finally, they are safe from tomb robbers.

The mummies of Egypt have given us a great gift—the gift of knowledge. The writings, paintings, and treasures of the mummies’ tombs teach us how the ancient Egyptians lived, how they worked, and how they played.

This knowledge helps bring the distant past alive. The ancient Egyptians believed they could live forever. In a way, they have.

## Doing More Research

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 77](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-077-img-01.jpg)

There’s a lot more you can learn about mummies, pyramids, and the people of ancient Egypt. The fun of research is seeing how many different sources you can explore.

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 77](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-077-img-02.jpg)

Most libraries and bookstores have lots of books about ancient Egypt.

Here are some things to remember when you’re using books for research:

1. You don’t have to read the whole book. Check the table of contents and the index to find the topics you’re interested in.

2. Write down the name of the book. When you take notes, make sure you write down the name of the book in your notebook so you can find it again.

3. Never copy exactly from a book. When you learn something new from a book, put it in your own words.

4. Make sure the book is nonfiction. Some books tell make-believe stories about mummies. Make-believe stories are called fiction. They’re fun to read, but not good for research.

Research books have facts and tell true stories. They are called nonfiction. A librarian or teacher can help you make sure the books you use for research are nonfiction.

Here are some good nonfiction books about mummies and ancient Egypt:

- Ancient Egypt, a DK Publishing Eyewitness book • The Ancient Egyptians, History Opens Windows series, by Jane Shuter • Ancient Egyptians and Their Neighbors: An Activity Guide by Marian Broida • If I Were a Kid in Ancient Egypt, Children of the Ancient World series, a

<!-- PDF page 78 -->

Cobblestone Publishing book • Mummies Made in Egypt by Aliki

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 78](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-078-img-01.jpg)

Many museums have exhibits on ancient Egypt. They can help you learn more about mummies and pyramids.

When you go to a museum:

1. Be sure to take your notebook! Write down anything that catches your interest. Draw pictures, too!

2. Ask questions. There are almost always people at museums who can help you find what you’re looking for.

3. Check the calendar. Many museums have special events and activities just for kids!

Here are some museums with exhibits about ancient Egypt: • Brooklyn Museum of Art (New York) • Denver Museum of Nature and Science • Field Museum of Natural History (Chicago) • Los Angeles County Museum of Art • Metropolitan Museum of Art (New York) • University of Memphis Institute of Egyptian Art and Archaeology

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 78](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-078-img-02.jpg)

There are some great nonfiction DVDs about ancient Egypt. As with books, make sure the DVDs you watch for research are nonfiction!

Check your library or video store for these and other nonfiction titles about ancient Egypt:

- Ancient Egypt Unearthed from Discovery Channel • Egypt: Secrets of the Pharaohs from National Geographic • Into the Great Pyramid from National Geographic • Mummies: Secrets of the Pharaohs from IMAX

## About the Author

![Illustration or page artwork from PDF page 92](/books/MagicTreeHouse-3/assets/page-092-img-01.jpg)

Will Osborne and Mary Pope Osborne have been married for a number of years and live in northwestern Connecticut with their three dogs, Joey, Mr. Bezo, and Little Bear.

Mary is the author of over one hundred books for children, including novels, picture books, biographies, and retellings of fairy tales and world mythologies. Will has worked for many years in the theater as an actor, director, and playwright. Together he and Mary have coauthored two books of Greek mythology and eight Magic Tree House® Fact Trackers. Will has also written a multimedia planetarium show, Magic Tree House Space Mission, and is cocreator with Randy Courts of Magic Tree House: The Musical, a full-scale Broadway-style family musical based on Christmas in Camelot. fiction titles, as well as many more books for kids.
